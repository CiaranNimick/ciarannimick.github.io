CWRU Motorsports · Suspension

Baja SAE rear upright

Developing and validating the 2027 Rear Upright using assembly-level load cases, ANSYS submodeling, and wheel-force inputs.

BAJA SAERear uprightCAD · FEA · Validation
Open projectClose project

01 / Design context

Optimizing for space

The upright connects the suspension, shafts, and wheel into one tightly packaged system. The project addresses geometry and clearance requirements while balancing stiffness, mass, serviceability, and manufacturability.

02 / Structural analysis

Analyzing the part in context

I am building the analysis around the complete rear outboard assembly—including the rim, hub, and upright—then using ANSYS submodeling to isolate the upright and provide loading conditions for downstream components. Direct wheel-force-transducer load cases help represent the forces the component sees on the vehicle.

CWRU Motorsports · Suspension

Front lower suspension arm

Defining requirements, iterating parametric CAD, and validating a lighter, more serviceable Baja SAE component.

Open projectClose project

01 / Requirements

Designing from vehicle goals

I’m responsible for the research, development, validation, and manufacturing of the front lower suspension arm for CWRU Motorsports. I translated vehicle-level goals into requirements focused on increased ground clearance, a 10–20% weight reduction, improved maintenance, and practical manufacturing.

02 / Design cycle

Parametric iteration and validation

I reviewed previous CWRU Motorsports designs and concepts from other Baja SAE teams, generated and selected concepts, then built parametric Siemens NX models so the design could evolve as suspension geometry was finalized. I’m using FEA to validate strength while continuing to remove unnecessary mass.

03

CWRU Motorsports · Test systems

CVT dynamometer

Designing a safe thermal enclosure and an FEA-validated gearbox-to-motor adapter for reliable testing.

Open projectClose project

01 / Design

Building a safer test system

Our car uses a custom CVT that needs dynamometer testing for performance characterization. As a first-year project, I designed a protective heat shroud to improve the system’s thermal performance and protect users from the heat generated while dissipating up to 20 kW of braking energy.

02 / Analysis

Connecting the gearbox to the motor

I designed a custom trilobe adapter matching the gearbox output and used Ansys FEA to verify it could withstand operating torque loads for secure, reliable integration.

03 / Manufacturing

From sheet metal to machined adapter

We waterjet-cut, bent, and welded the sheet-metal shroud. The adapter was turned to size on a manual lathe, then CNC machined to create the trilobe profile and offset holes.
